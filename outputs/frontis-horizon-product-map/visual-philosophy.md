# Boundary Engine

Boundary Engine treats an enterprise AI platform as a precise field of thresholds rather than a pile of feature cards. The composition is built from hard-edged strata: entry, self, expert network, evolution engine, and business ground. Each stratum should feel engineered, measured, and intentionally placed, with the horizon line acting as the quiet signal that capability expands as the organization advances.

The visual language is deliberately restrained: quantum black, white, structural gray, transition cyan, and rare energy orange. Cyan is the boundary marker and routing signal. Orange appears only where motion, ignition, or compounding value must be felt. The final work must look meticulously crafted, as if every line weight, label, and spacing decision was refined by someone at the top of the field.

Information lives primarily through spatial hierarchy. ME is the human-facing control layer, WE is the collaborative expert field, and MA is the engine below the visible work. Text is compact and essential, used as technical labeling rather than explanation. The diagram should reward close reading, but the whole architecture must be understood at first glance.

The craft standard is a boardroom-grade technical artifact: clean enough for a product launch deck, specific enough for sales enablement, and polished enough to stand alone as a brand asset. It should avoid decorative softness, gradients, and shadows. Depth comes from layer order, rule lines, top borders, and controlled contrast.

The subtle reference is the shift from scattered AI tools to an AI-native organization. The viewer should sense that tools become people-facing identity, identity commands expert capability, expert capability feeds an evolution engine, and the engine returns intelligence to the whole system. Nothing should feel generic; every layer must point back to Frontis Horizon as a system that grows through use.
